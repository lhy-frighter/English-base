## Section A 仔细阅读
Recommendation algorithms are built to please. By tracking every click, pause and scroll, a video platform can predict, with unsettling accuracy, which message will hold a user's attention for the next thirty seconds. The result is a feed that feels almost clairvoyant — and a growing body of research warns that it also narrows the mind.

The mechanism is known as the "filter bubble". When users engage with a claim, the system infers agreement and serves more of the same, while opposing views, which would slow engagement, quietly disappear from the feed. What makes the effect insidious is its invisibility: unlike a newspaper with an obvious editorial line, a personalised feed presents itself as "for you", a neutral mirror of personal taste rather than an editor with opinions. Users consequently mistake an amplified slice of the world for the world itself.

Critics of this thesis rightly note that users are not prisoners. People maintain group chats, follow diverse accounts and occasionally click opposing views out of curiosity. Yet the burden of correction should not be overstated. Breaking a filter bubble requires active, repeated effort, whereas remaining inside it requires none at all — and platforms' revenues reward the effortless option. The practical lesson is not that algorithms are evil but that attention is a cultivated resource: deliberately following sources one disagrees with, and scheduling periods when the feed is simply switched off, restores some of the serendipity that personalisation quietly removes.

### Q1
Recommendation algorithms narrow users' minds mainly by ____.
- A) showing them a great variety of opposing views
- B) repeatedly serving content aligned with their engagement
- C) slowing down page-loading on purpose
- D) publishing obvious newspaper editorials
> answer: B
> analysis: 第二段用户一旦互动，系统推断其认同并推送更多同类内容，对立观点因降低停留而消失，这就是收窄心智的机制。
> point: 细节题

### Q2
Why is the filter bubble described as "insidious"?
- A) It is clearly labelled as editorial opinion.
- B) It operates invisibly while appearing neutral.
- C) Users can never access outside news.
- D) It forces users into group chats.
> answer: B
> analysis: insidious 对应 its invisibility——个性化推荐伪装成"为你推荐"的中立镜像，而非有立场的编辑，用户难以察觉。
> point: 词义/细节题

### Q3
Critics of the filter-bubble thesis argue that ____.
- A) algorithms imprison users completely
- B) users can and do encounter diverse views elsewhere
- C) platforms should remove all personalisation
- D) curiosity is disappearing among users
> answer: B
> analysis: 第三段 users are not prisoners，人们有群聊、关注多元账号、出于好奇点开对立观点，说明用户并非完全被困。

### Q4
The author thinks escaping the bubble is difficult because ____.
- A) it demands active effort while staying inside needs none
- B) opposing views are legally banned
- C) group chats discourage diversity
- D) algorithms pay users to leave the feed
> answer: A
> analysis: 打破气泡需要主动、反复的努力，留在里面却毫不费力，而平台收入奖励毫不费力的那一方。

The author's practical recommendation is to ____.
- A) stop using the Internet altogether
- B) deliberately diversify sources and take breaks from the feed

> answer: B
> analysis: 末句明确建议：主动关注自己不认同的信源、安排关闭信息流的时段，找回被个性化抹掉的偶遇。

### Q6
Directions: For this part, you are allowed 30 minutes to write an essay commenting on the remark "Attention is a cultivated resource." You can cite examples. Write at least 150 words but no more than 200 words.
> model: The remark that attention is a cultivated resource captures a truth the smartphone age makes easy to forget: what we consistently attend to shapes what we understand, desire and eventually become.
> Attention is not a fixed tank refilled each morning but a muscle trained by habit. Every interrupted train of thought lowers the threshold for the next distraction, while sustained reading, writing or conversation gradually rebuilds the capacity for deep focus. Recommendation systems exploit this asymmetry ruthlessly, because fragmented attention is easier to sell than calm attention. Recognising this does not require rejecting technology; it requires designing its place in life. Fixed periods without notifications, a single task foregrounded on the screen, and deliberately slow reading of long articles are small disciplines with compounding returns. A student who protects two uninterrupted hours each evening will, within a semester, outperform a peer of equal talent who studies amid constant pings.
> In the end, managing attention is managing the self. Those who decide in advance what deserves their hours will find that focus, once cultivated, is not only a study advantage but a form of independence.
